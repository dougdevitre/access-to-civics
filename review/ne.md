## Nebraska Constitution, Article III, Section 1

**Official heading:** Legislative authority; how vested; power of initiative; power of referendum
**URN:** `urn:const:us:ne:art-03:sec-01`

### The clause, word for word

> The legislative authority of the state shall be vested in a Legislature consisting of one chamber. The people reserve for themselves the power to propose laws and amendments to the Constitution and to enact or reject the same at the polls, independent of the Legislature, which power shall be called the power of initiative. The people also reserve power at their own option to approve or reject at the polls any act, item, section, or part of any act passed by the Legislature, which power shall be called the power of referendum.

### Plain language — ages 8–10

> Nebraska's lawmakers are one group, not two. Nebraska is the only state that does it this way. The people kept two powers too. They can make a law by voting. They can also undo one.

### Plain language — ages 11–14

> Nebraska puts its lawmaking power in a Legislature of one chamber. It is the only state that does. The same section keeps two powers for the people. Initiative lets them propose a law and pass it at the polls. Referendum lets them approve or reject a law the Legislature passed.

## Recording a review

Sign-off lives in the seed data, not in this file. For each gloss you have checked, edit `data/seed/ne/glosses.json` and set `reviewed_by` to your name or your committee's, and `reviewed_at` to the date. For the sensitivity calls, edit the `reviewed_by` and `reviewed_at` at the top of `data/seed/clause-sensitivity.json`.

Then re-run `npm run ingest -- --state NE --write` and `npm run review:status`. The app's grown-ups page reports the count, so a signed-off corpus says so in public and an unreviewed one says that too.

If something is wrong, open an issue rather than editing the gloss in place — the disagreement is worth keeping. We will credit you unless you would rather we did not.
